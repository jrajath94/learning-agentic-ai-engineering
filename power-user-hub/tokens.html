Frontier labs

Nobody in the opened record says 5 to 10 thousand tokens a day.

The figure is too small for the workflows these same people describe. What the transcripts and the August 2025 study do record is spend, parallel sessions, and a share of the workday.

The 5 to 10 thousand figure

It is not in the sources this pass opened. A coding agent that reads a repo and runs tests spends that many tokens in a single tool result. Treating 5 to 10 thousand tokens as a frontier-lab day would be low by orders of magnitude next to the numbers that are written down.

What is written down, with the speaker and the time:

  • 7 May 2025, about 13:50. Boris Cherny: Claude Code is free internally. Transcript mirror of Latent Space.
  • Same episode, about 14:48. Cat Wu: external active users were around $6 per day, a bit higher across a month than a $20 Cursor subscription. She also said the tool is aimed at being snappy and thorough, not at minimizing tokens.
  • Same episode, about 13:18. Boris: some people at Anthropic had been racking up thousands of dollars a day with automation. Most people were not. He had just described starting one Claude per lint violation, up to about 1,000.
  • Same episode, about 1:04. Cat Wu: there was no separate subscription yet. Pay as you go kept the upfront commitment at zero. So the $6 figure is from the API-priced period, not from a later bundled plan.
  • 28 Sep 2026 Latent Space page, about 00:52. Thariq Shihipar: before Claude Code, people were not used to spending more than $20 a month. Opus 4 was expensive. Opus 4.5 was, in his words, both great and cheap. The host's line about 15 Claude Code accounts is the host's line.
  • YouTube page text for the Boris interview, no caption file: the speaker says about five at a time on a computer, and most nights hundreds, sometimes thousands, in parallel, at the edge of a max plan, with the API as overflow. Speaker labels are missing. Medium confidence. yt-dlp could not save captions.
  • August 2025 Anthropic study: 132 engineers and researchers surveyed, 53 interviewed, 200,000 Claude Code transcripts from February and August 2025. Self-report: Claude in 59 percent of work and a 50 percent productivity gain, up from 28 percent and 20 percent a year earlier. They also report a 67 percent increase in merged pull requests per engineer per day after adopting Claude Code. No token total per person is in that page.

The internal-free line and the thousands-of-dollars line sit a minute apart. The page does not say whether those dollars were an internal transfer price or a real invoice. Both sentences are quoted. The tension is left open.

How a large token bill actually gets spent

These are the mechanisms named by the people above, not a guessed stack.

  1. Parallel sessions, separate work. Five on one computer in the later interview text. The 2 Jan 2026 post already said five local checkouts plus five to ten on the web. The May 2025 episode adds the extreme form: one agent per lint, and some people running that wide enough to look like thousands of dollars a day.
  2. Overnight fan-out. The interview page text says most nights, not every night, hundreds in parallel. That is a scheduled burst, not a longer chat.
  3. Compaction instead of a bigger window. Boris, about 8:58 in the May episode: after truncation and rewriting old tool calls, compaction that worked was asking Claude to summarize the previous messages. Cat Wu, about 55:53: compacting several times weakens the original intent. Long runs spend tokens twice, once to work and once to forget carefully.
  4. Verification, which the August study says is still mostly human. More than half of respondents could fully delegate only 0 to 20 percent of their work. They debug and reread because they did not write the code. That reread is part of the cost, and it is also why a token total is not a productivity total.
  5. Effort routed by task. Thariq, about 00:25: high or max effort for code review and security, low or medium for UI. High effort is more tokens on purpose.

Synthesis, not a measurement

Stay inside one subscription until the product's own usage screen says you are at the cap. The reproducible loop is still: one bug, one failing test, one diff you read. Add a second checkout only when the first session is waiting on a long command. Add an overnight fan-out only for tasks that do not share files, the lint-sized shape Boris described, and only after you have watched one of them fail a test. If the cap arrives, the documented overflow is the API, which is how the May 2025 $6 average was produced. Do not buy a second account because a host joked about fifteen.
